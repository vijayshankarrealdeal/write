Scrivener vs InkSpaceX:
which one should you write in?

Both tools share a heritage — a Binder on the left, a draft in the middle, an Inspector on the right. The differences come down to where you write, how you sync, what you pay, and what your finished story does next. This page lays them out side by side, no marketing.

Updated May 2026 · 10 min read · Verified against Scrivener 3.4 / InkSpaceX 1.x

The short version

If you write on a Mac and only a Mac, ship books to Kindle every six months, and want maximum control over print layout: Scrivener. The Compile dialog is unmatched.

If you write across devices, want a free tier, want a story to be discoverable when it's done, want optional AI assistance, or you've ever lost work to a Dropbox sync conflict: InkSpaceX. Same drafting muscle, modern plumbing, no per-device license.

The honest trade-offs

Pick Scrivener if…

  • You write on macOS or Windows and that's it.
  • You ship print books with complex layouts (front matter, multiple title pages, custom indents per chapter).
  • You've already invested in a Compile preset that you love.
  • You prefer one-time purchases over any subscription on principle.
  • You're 100% comfortable with Dropbox sync's "close before switch" rule.

Pick InkSpaceX if…

  • You write across devices — phone on the bus, laptop at the café, desktop at home.
  • You use Linux, Chromebook, or Android for any part of your workflow.
  • You want a free tier that isn't a 30-day clock.
  • You want your finished story to be discoverable, not exported into a void.
  • You want optional AI co-writing without it being shoved at you.
  • You want real-time sync that doesn't ask you to "close before switching."
  • You're starting fresh and don't have a Scrivener investment to protect.
