Sudowrite vs InkSpaceX:
two different bets on AI for fiction.

Sudowrite is an AI-first tool that wraps a generative model in a writer's chrome. InkSpaceX is a writer-first tool with optional AI assist on top of a real long-form editor. They look similar in screenshots; in practice they're built for different writers. Here is which is which.

Updated May 2026 · 9 min read

The short version

If your blocker is staring at a blank page and you want a model to keep generating until you find a thread to grab, Sudowrite. Their generative cards (Describe, Brainstorm, Twist, Canvas) are designed for that loop and they're good at it.

If you have an actual draft to manage — chapters, scenes, a binder, multiple devices, version history, footnotes, publish — and you want AI as a tool you reach for occasionally, not the centerpiece, InkSpaceX. The drafting layer is closer to Scrivener; the AI is closer to a co-pilot button.

The honest trade-offs

Pick Sudowrite if…

  • You're stuck on a blank page and want generative momentum.
  • You like the brainstorming + cards + canvas idea-fountain workflow.
  • You want a story bible that the AI actively reads from.
  • You're comfortable web-only and online-only.
  • You're okay paying ~$19–29/month for an AI-first product.

Pick InkSpaceX if…

  • You have a real long-form draft to organise (binder, scenes, snapshots).
  • You want AI as a button you press occasionally, not the centerpiece.
  • You want native iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, Linux apps + offline.
  • You want a free tier that isn't a credit clock.
  • You want your finished story to be discoverable by readers, not just exported.
  • You write nonfiction or anything with footnotes (Sudowrite doesn't have them).

Can you use both?

Yes, and many writers do. A common pattern: brainstorm and generate raw passages in Sudowrite when you're stuck, then paste them into InkSpaceX where the long-form structure lives. InkSpaceX exports cleanly to .docx so the round-trip works.

If you only want one, ask yourself this: in a typical session, are you wrestling with structure (where does this scene go, what does the character know yet, did I already use this metaphor) or with blank page (I have nothing here, I need a starting point)? Structure → InkSpaceX. Blank page → Sudowrite.
