Why writers leave Scrivener

Scrivener is a great tool for one thing it does very, very well: organising a long manuscript on a desktop. It has earned a generation of writers' loyalty for good reason. But the world it was designed for — one writer, one Mac, one local Documents folder — is shrinking. Three friction points come up over and over:

  • No real web app, no real Android app. Scrivener for iOS exists and is good. The desktop apps are mature. There is no first-party Android app and no in-browser editor. If your manuscript moves between a Linux laptop, a Chromebook, and an Android phone, you are left with file-shuffling.
  • The Dropbox sync model is showing its age. Two devices opening the same project at once corrupts it. The team-recommended workflow is "always close before switching devices" — which is a mid-2000s constraint dressed up as a feature.
  • One-time license, per platform. Mac and Windows are separate purchases. iOS is a third. There is no free tier, so trying it before you commit means a 30-day clock.

None of this means Scrivener is bad — it means it was designed for a different decade. If those frictions are why you're reading this, the rest of the page is for you.

Honest trade-offs

Switching tools is a real cost. We'd rather you go in clear-eyed than oversell. Here is where each one is genuinely better.

Where InkSpaceX wins

  • One account, every device. No Dropbox config.
  • Native Android + browser apps Scrivener doesn't have.
  • Free tier covers the entire writing experience.
  • Real-time sync — open the same chapter on phone and laptop, see live changes.
  • Built-in reader + publishing — your finished story has somewhere to land.
  • AI co-writer (opt-in) for blank-page rescues.
  • Auto-snapshot before AI edits so regrets are one click away.

Where Scrivener still wins

  • Compile dialog is genuinely more powerful for complex print layouts.
  • Twenty years of plugins, scripts, and writer-shared templates.
  • Project Bookmarks UI for project-level resources is more polished.
  • One-time purchase model if you object to subscriptions on principle (InkSpaceX Pro is monthly; the free tier remains free).
  • If you're 100% offline, 100% Mac-only, and shipping a print manuscript every quarter — Scrivener is still excellent at exactly that.

Migrating from Scrivener

The five-minute path:

  1. In Scrivener, Compile your project to .docx. File → Compile → choose "Microsoft Word (.docx)". Pick "Original" formatting if you want chapter breaks preserved.
  2. Sign up at inkspacex.com/signup — no credit card. Free.
  3. Create a new writing, then drag your .docx onto the binder. Each Heading 1 in the .docx becomes a top-level chapter; Heading 2 becomes a scene under it. Footnotes and inline formatting come over intact.
  4. Open the Inspector on each chapter and paste your Scrivener synopsis into the Synopsis tab. (We're working on a native .scriv project importer; for now this is the cleanest path.)
  5. Pin the editor on every device you write on. Sign in once. The same project is there.

Snapshots from Scrivener don't migrate automatically. If you have important historical versions you want to preserve, copy each into its own scene before compile. We're tracking native snapshot import — drop us a line if it would unblock your switch.

Common questions

Is InkSpaceX really a Scrivener replacement, or just inspired by it?

It's an honest replacement for the daily drafting workflow — Binder, three views, Inspector, Scrivenings, Page view, snapshots, footnotes, find & replace, spell check, word goals, smart punctuation. We've intentionally not rebuilt Scrivener's Compile dialog (we replaced it with a one-click publish flow). If your workflow depends on a specific Compile preset, that's the place to test before committing.

Can I keep using Scrivener and InkSpaceX in parallel?

Yes — many writers do during a transition. Compile from Scrivener to .docx, drop into InkSpaceX for any device that isn't your Mac, and edit in either. Just don't try to round-trip the same chapter through Scrivener's Compile + InkSpaceX's import + back to Scrivener; you'll lose history.

Where does my work live?

Encrypted at rest on our servers and locally on each device you sign in to. You can export everything as Markdown, .docx, PDF, or ePub at any time, or delete your account in two clicks (the data leaves our database within 24 hours).

Do you train AI on my writing?

No. Your drafts and published work are not used to train any AI model — ours, or anyone else's. The Pro AI co-writer runs against isolated providers under strict no-retention contracts.
